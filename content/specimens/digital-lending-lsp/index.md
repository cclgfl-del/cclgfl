The Reserve Bank of India's framework for digital lending, first set out in guidelines issued in 2022, proceeds from a simple premise: a regulated entity that lends through a digital platform remains responsible for what that platform does. Lending service providers act on the lender's behalf, and the lender answers for them.

The premise is sound. Its effectiveness depends on the borrower being able to identify the regulated entity behind the app — which is why disclosure of the lender's identity at the point of sale, and a key fact statement before the loan is made, sit at the centre of the framework rather than at its edges.

A rule that allocates responsibility correctly does little if the person harmed cannot tell whom to hold responsible. The framework's disclosure requirements are therefore not ancillary to its liability rules; they are what make those rules usable.
