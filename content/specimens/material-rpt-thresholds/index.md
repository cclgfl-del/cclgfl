Regulation 23 of the [SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015](https://www.sebi.gov.in/) requires that material related party transactions of a listed entity be approved by its shareholders, with the related parties not voting. The effect of the rule therefore turns almost entirely on where materiality begins.

## I. The change in the threshold

Until 2022 a transaction was material if it exceeded ten per cent of the listed entity's annual consolidated turnover. For the largest companies, that percentage translated into very large absolute sums, so significant transactions with related parties could proceed without a shareholder vote. The threshold was amended to the lower of an absolute figure and the percentage.

| | Materiality threshold |
|---|---|
| Before April 2022 | 10% of annual consolidated turnover |
| From April 2022 | ₹1,000 crore or 10% of annual consolidated turnover, whichever is lower |

## II. Who the change actually reached

Because the new test takes the lower of the two figures, it changes nothing for a company whose ten per cent of turnover is already below ₹1,000 crore. The amendment bites only on companies large enough that ten per cent of their turnover exceeds that figure. It is, in substance, a rule about the largest listed groups.

That is a reasonable target. It does, however, mean that the question of whether minority shareholders are adequately protected in smaller listed companies was not addressed by the change, and remains a matter of the percentage test and of audit committee oversight.

## III. The audit committee's role

Every related party transaction, material or not, requires prior approval of the audit committee, and only independent directors on the committee may vote on it. The committee is therefore the principal safeguard below the materiality line, which places weight on the quality of the information it receives and on the independence of those deciding.
