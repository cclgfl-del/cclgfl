Section 149(6) of the [Companies Act, 2013](https://www.mca.gov.in/) sets out who may be appointed as an independent director, largely by listing relationships that disqualify: pecuniary relationships with the company, family relationships with promoters and directors, and recent employment or professional engagement. The Listing Regulations add parallel criteria for listed entities.

These are tests of the past. They ask whether a candidate has been too close to the company. They do not ask whether the candidate, once appointed, is likely to act independently of the management that proposed them — which is the only question that matters to the shareholders the office exists to protect.

That forward-looking judgment is left to the nomination and remuneration committee and to the board. It is worth asking whether the disclosures made at appointment give shareholders enough to assess it for themselves.
